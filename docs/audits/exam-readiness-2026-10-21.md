# Lesson exam-readiness audit

21 October 2026. Scope: lesson content only. The 80-question assignment bank and cheat sheets are unchanged. No PYQs added.

## Method and boundaries

- Read all 80 assignment questions and all 326 answer options against the 135 topic explanations, including distractors and matching-question mappings. The machine-readable companion records the semantic finding for each question and option. Source/fact-ID mapping alone was not treated as proof of coverage.
- Reviewed the text of all 40 original slide decks and targeted passages across the 40 caption files, including mobile-generation classification, low-cost validation, healthcare delivery, bar codes, ASPIRE, patent disclosure, incubator stay and funding. This was not a verbatim second reading of every caption. Source file hashes and portal URLs are recorded in the source inventory.
- Re-read the eight supplied official assignment solution PDFs and checked their highlighted selections against the bank.
- Checked lesson visuals for useful relationships, labels, categories and source/key contradictions. Ran all topics through the UI in both themes at 320, 360, 390, 768 and 1280 pixels. Inspected corrected visual examples as rendered images.
- Searched for genuine previous-year proctored-exam questions separately. No verified exam paper found. Public practice pages found were based on weekly assignments, including 2019/2023 assignments, not authenticated proctored-exam papers. This is not evidence that no PYQs exist anywhere.
- An assignment key is evidence of its accepted answer, not proof that every rejected proposition is universally false. Where sources disagree, lessons now label the conflict instead of silently teaching the key as general fact.

These are 16 finding clusters, not 16 wrong quiz questions. Some are missing discriminators or misleading visuals rather than wrong answer selections. The corrected lesson text supports all 80 bank questions, with known key ambiguities flagged. That does not certify the unknown final exam.

## Bank issues deliberately left unchanged

The task restriction was lesson-only fixes. These existing explanations deserve caution:

- W2 Q3: treating a definition-only key as proof against the Guilford attribution.
- W5 Q3: huge financial investment is not a universal requirement of every technological innovation.
- W5 Q4: the selected list is narrower than the broader process discussed in lectures.
- W7 Q9: the question does not specify all filing/disclosure conditions, and no patent is not the same as no IP protection of any kind.
- W8 Q8: the angel answer conflicts with the lecture's self-first sequence; a weekly assignment key is not a guarantee of a future exam's answer.
